## How the solver shaped the content

The solver flagged 13 rooms that didn't play as designed. Each was redesigned, not papered over by changing par to match:

- **Unsolvable or too deep:** c2-05, c2-11, c2-16, c3-05, c3-06, c3-08, c3-09 and c3-10 (geometry out of reach, a step that landed in a lane, or a climb too tall to search).
- **Zero-reflect bypass:** c2-06, c2-07 and c2-12. A recurring cause: an emitter's first shot comes one full period after room start, so a fast player could race through. The fix was holding pens and earlier streams.
- **Under par:** c2-15, c3-03 and (after its redesign) c3-10. Two solver routes were better than my design, and I adopted them: **c2-15 Sluice** (walk in the weight's wake) and **c3-10** (one well-timed stone).

### Solver changes in M2

- **Deep fallback.** When the 5/4/3 Hz searches hit the state cap, the gate retries at 4 Hz, then 5 Hz, with a 2.5M cap. The Examiner phases need it.
- **Recorded-solution fallback (GDD line 505).** A room too deep to search passes if its recorded solution clears within par at a human input rate (≤ 5 changes in any second). It is flagged `⚑ proven by its recorded solution`.
- **Staged solving for tools.** `solve({ prefix, until })` searches one core or one climb at a time. This is how Examiner III of Chapter 3 and c3-08 Tower were solved.
